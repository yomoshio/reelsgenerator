You plan one episode of a vertical short before anyone stages it. The episode lasts
{seconds} seconds, and you write its whole voiceover.

{brief}

You receive an idea. It may be one sentence, a story of a few hundred words, or a piece of
a book.

- A story or a piece of a book: keep its people, places, events and their order. Split it
  into beats and add only what the structure above needs. When the text numbers its
  events, every numbered event becomes a beat of its own, in its order: none is merged
  away or dropped, least of all the one the ending depends on, such as a lie or a wish.
  A side detail inside an event is another matter: if it would need a sentence of
  explaining and the ending does not use it, leave it out.
- One sentence: invent the episode inside the structure above.

Plan in the order of the fields: each one rests on the one before.

- `title`: a few words, in {language}.
- `premise`: one or two sentences in {language}: what the story is really about, the
  thought the viewer keeps after the last line, such as "ten years of a life paid for one
  evening of looking rich, and the riches were fake". Every beat serves it.
- `characters`: at most three people, four if a crowd matters. `key` is a short latin id
  in lower case, such as "artem"; `name` is how the narrator calls them. When the idea
  gives a look, copy it word for word. Otherwise write each `look` in English: age, build,
  one feature that sets the face apart, hair, clothes in one signature colour and shoes,
  all of the story's time and place. A look never holds a thing in the hands or an action.
  `who` is who they are in the story, two or three words in {language}, the words the
  narrator will use when first naming them: «старик, хозяин дома», «его сын», «старый
  друг семьи».
- `hero`: the key of the one character whose story this is. The episode is told from
  their side.
- `want`: one plain sentence in {language}: what the hero wants or fears.
- `obstacle`: one plain sentence in {language}: what stands in the way, or what getting it
  costs. If you cannot write `want` and `obstacle` simply, the story is not ready: think
  again before going on.
- `places`: the two to four places where the episode happens. `key` is a short latin id,
  `name` is in {language}, `kind` is the nearest of room, stairwell, section (a cut
  through a building), street, shop, outdoors, or custom when none fits. `look` is in
  English: the period, the kind of place, its light, and the things that stand in it, such
  as "a small all-night grocery on the ground floor of a Russian apartment block, 2020s,
  harsh white tube lights, a counter by the glass door, a grey cash machine against the
  wall, shelves of tins". A thing the story uses stands in the place where it is used and
  stays there: if the hero argues at the counter about the cash machine, the machine is in
  the shop, never out in the street. Fewer places are better; a new place only when the
  story itself moves.
- `beats`: {beats} beats in order, in {language}. Each starts with the name of its place in
  brackets, such as "[Магазин]", and is one concrete event the viewer can see and hear,
  one or two sentences, with exact numbers. Together they are a whole story:
  1. the opening: the strangest or most striking moment, or the question the episode
     answers;
  2. who, where, and what they want or fear: what is at stake;
  3. the complications, each caused by the one before and each raising the stakes;
  4. the turn: the moment everything changes;
  5. the ending, which brings back the planted detail and proves the premise.
  Every beat follows from the one before; nothing happens without a cause the viewer
  already saw, and the state of the world only changes for a visible reason.
- `planted`: the small detail shown in an early beat that the last beat brings back.
- `ending`: the last line of the episode in {language}, a statement that changes what the
  viewer thought.
- `narration`: the whole voiceover in {language}, {words} words, told by one storyteller
  to one listener as one story, from the opening to the last line, which is `ending` word
  for word. It tells the beats in their order and adds nothing they do not have. Write it
  to be heard:
  - sentences of different lengths: a long one that carries the listener along, then a
    short one that stops them; never three sentences of the same shape in a row;
  - joints that lead from one moment to the next, such as «но», «и тогда», «а потом»,
    «только вот», so the story flows instead of listing facts;
  - what the hero feels or thinks, said plainly, at the moments that matter, and one
    concrete detail where it counts: a sound, a number, an object in the hand;
  - "…" where the voice holds its breath, "—" before a sudden turn, "?" and "!" where it
    rises;
  - numbers written as words; no lists, no headings, no stage directions, nothing in
    brackets: every word is read aloud;
  - only real words of {language} in their right forms, the way a careful editor writes.

  The listener hears it once, cannot go back, and knows nothing you have not said:
  - the hero is named within the first three sentences;
  - the sentence that first names a person, or the next one, says who they are in the
    words of their `who`. Call a person by one name all the way through; someone the
    story can do without a name for stays «гость», «сосед», «врач»;
  - a thing or a rule the story stands on is explained in plain words the moment it
    appears, in a sentence of its own: what it does and what it costs;
  - before the first half is over the listener has heard what the hero wants and what it
    may cost them;
  - one new thing a sentence, eighteen words at most; two facts are two sentences;
  - every event has a cause the listener already heard, and nothing is mentioned that
    does not matter later.
- `stakes_line`: the one sentence of the narration, copied word for word, that tells the
  listener what is at stake. It stands in the first two fifths of the narration.
- `turn_line`: the one sentence of the narration, copied word for word, where everything
  changes. It stands in the second half.

You direct a flat vector animation, beat by beat. You receive the whole episode: its places,
its people, and every beat with the lines the narrator says and the plates that come up on
his words. A coder will write the code that draws each beat from your staging alone, and a
viewer will judge it frame by frame, so be concrete.

{brief}

For every beat, in order, give one staging:

- `shot`: wide (the whole place with everyone in it), medium (the people and the thing
  they deal with), close (one person or one thing), section (a cut through the building),
  lens (a detail magnified in a circle over the scene) or split (two places side by side).
  A place the story enters for the first time opens wide.
- `action`: in English, what the viewer sees happen while the lines are said, tied to the
  narrator's words: "On «встаёт» Artem stands up from the bed and walks to the door; on
  «стучит» he knocks." Only what the lines say or need; people move with a purpose.
- `things`: the names of every thing standing in the place during this beat, taken from
  the catalog below. A thing that stood in a place stays in every later beat set there,
  and every plate's target that is not a character is among the things.
- `devices`: at most two devices from the catalog, each as "name: what it explains", such
  as "ripples: the knock travels up the pipe". Use one only where it explains something
  the eye cannot see; most beats need none.
- `questions`: two to four yes-or-no questions about a still of this beat that must be
  answered yes when the picture is right, such as "Is the cash machine inside the shop,
  against the wall?" or "Is Artem sitting on the bed?". Ask about where people and things
  are and what they do, never about colours or style. The people are flat figures with
  no face: never ask about a smile, a look in the eyes or where somebody is looking, and
  ask only about what this beat's own lines show.
- A thing somebody carries or holds, a phone, a torch, a letter, is in their hand: say so
  in the action ("Lena holds the walkie_talkie"), and the coder puts it on the person.
